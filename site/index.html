Why Six Harmony

Deadlines don't slip all at once.
They slip a little at a time.

I've worked industrial jobs — furnace work, plumbing before that. I never once watched a deadline die from one big disaster. It was always an accumulation: the part nobody reordered. The tweak a customer called in that never made it to paper. The emergency that pulled two people off a job mid-task.

Every one of those enters the business the same way — a phone call, a note, a follow-up someone meant to get to. Office work nobody has hands for when everyone's covering the floor. It doesn't look like a crisis on any single day. It just quietly becomes a missed deadline, a mad scramble, a customer who doesn't call back.

That's what we install against. The receptionist catches what comes in. The CRM writes it down where it can't be lost. The agent chases what would otherwise be forgotten. Nothing heroic — just nothing dropped.

Fair questions

Does it sound like a robot?

Judge for yourself: talk to the sample receptionist for eye care or industrial. It handles interruptions, and callers can ask things the way they normally would.

What happens when it can't answer something?

It hands off. Clinical questions, pricing it isn't allowed to quote, or anything outside its lane goes to a person on your team with a written summary. It's set up to hand off rather than guess.

We already have a practice system or CRM. Do we have to switch?

Not necessarily. We work alongside what you already run, and every integration is confirmed during scope — before anything is built.

Do I keep my phone number?

Yes. Your number stays yours. The system sits behind it, so patients and customers call the same number they always have.

Is the demo a real business?

No. Every demo runs on a fictional business with sample data. Yours is branded and configured to how your team already works.
